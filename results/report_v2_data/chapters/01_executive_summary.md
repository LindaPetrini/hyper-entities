## Executive Summary

A hyper-entity is a coherent future system that doesn't exist yet but is already reorganizing investment, research, and institutions around its anticipated existence. At the far end of the spectrum, a Dyson sphere has shaped scientific thinking for decades without anyone coming close to building it. [Fusion energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_power) in the 1970s, the [Human Genome Project](https://www.genome.gov/human-genome-project) in 1990, and AGI today are nearer-term examples; each reshaped priorities and created new fields years before producing results. Which systems playing this role right now deserve more attention than they're getting? This report identifies 39, drawn from [Existential Hope](https://existentialhope.com), Foresight Institute's initiative mapping positive long-term futures.

We analyzed 108 documents from the [Existential Hope](https://existentialhope.com) community (podcast transcripts, world gallery scenarios, and [AI pathways essays](https://ai-pathways.existentialhope.com/)) using an AI pipeline to extract and score candidates on hyper-entity qualification, technology impact, and [d/acc values alignment](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/11/27/dacc.html) (democratic, decentralized, defensive, differential acceleration). Two independent human reviewers curated the results to 39 consensus entities.

The clearest opportunities sit at the intersection of high impact and strong values alignment: [Automated Scientific Publishing](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/kAgJJa3HLSZxsuSrf/we-need-a-theory-of-anthropic-measure-binding) for machine consumers (enabling AI-native knowledge sharing), [LexCommons](https://lexdao.org/) (open-source legal infrastructure built on the early-stage work of [LexDAO](https://lexdao.org/)), and Competitive Governance Protocol Stacks (enabling fluid movement between jurisdictions and rulesets). A separate cluster ([brain-computer interfaces](https://neuralink.com/), [mind uploading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_uploading)) has transformative potential but needs governance frameworks that don't yet exist.

For those allocating capital, regulatory attention, or research priority, the relevant question is not only which technologies are most powerful, but which infrastructure prevents powerful technologies from becoming centralized bottlenecks. The entities in this report represent that second category: systems that make other breakthroughs safer, more distributed, and more likely to generate broad benefits. Many require long funding horizons and regulatory frameworks designed for experimentation. They warrant greater visibility in policy and investment conversations that currently orbit a much narrower set of technologies.
