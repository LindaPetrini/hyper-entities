## Executive Summary

**What are hyper-entities?** A hyper-entity is a coherent future system that doesn't exist yet but is already reshaping the present. Think of [fusion energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_power) in the 1970s, or the [Human Genome Project](https://www.genome.gov/human-genome-project) in 1990: ambitious technological systems that reorganized billions in investment, reshaped research priorities, and created new institutions years before producing results. They matter because they reveal where coordination is forming around shared technological futures, and because getting the *right* systems built could determine whether emerging technologies concentrate or distribute power. This report identifies 39 such entities, drawn from [Existential Hope](https://existentialhope.com), Foresight Institute's initiative mapping positive long-term futures, that we believe deserve attention, investment, and governance focus today.

**What we did and why.** We analyzed 108 documents: 65 [Existential Hope podcast](https://existentialhope.com/podcast) transcripts, 40 [world gallery](https://existentialhope.com) scenario submissions, and [essays on AI pathways](https://ai-pathways.existentialhope.com/) and long-term futures from the [Existential Hope](https://existentialhope.com) community. An AI pipeline extracted over 300 candidate systems (including some conceptual overlaps and near-duplicates), scored across three dimensions: Does this qualify as a hyper-entity? What's its potential technology impact? How well does it align with [d/acc values](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/11/27/dacc.html) (democratic, decentralized, defensive, differential acceleration)? Two independent reviewers curated these to 39 consensus entities. We undertook this work to map technological systems that could matter for human flourishing but receive less attention than today's dominant discourse: new manufacturing paradigms, truth verification systems, governance protocols that could prevent technological lock-in.

**What we found: A landscape of overlooked infrastructure.** The scatter plot positions 29 entities by values alignment and technology impact. The upper-right quadrant (high impact *and* strong d/acc alignment) contains the "sweet spot" systems: [Automated Scientific Publishing](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/kAgJJa3HLSZxsuSrf/we-need-a-theory-of-anthropic-measure-binding) for machine consumers (enabling AI-native knowledge sharing), [LexCommons](https://lexdao.org/) (open-source legal infrastructure, a vision being built on the early-stage work of [LexDAO](https://lexdao.org/)), and Competitive Governance Protocol Stacks (systems enabling fluid movement between jurisdictions and rulesets). The upper-left quadrant highlights powerful technologies needing urgent governance attention: [brain-computer interfaces](https://neuralink.com/), [mind uploading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_uploading), and other systems with high potential but uncertain power dynamics.

![Scatter plot: d/acc alignment vs technology impact](scatter_plot.png)

Most entities cluster in the mid-range, meaningful but not yet paradigm-shifting. This distribution suggests an emerging landscape where many systems remain in "foundational research" (11 entities) or "early demonstrations" (20 entities) stages, with only 2 near deployment. The most undervalued entities, those scoring high but receiving little attention, include [Chemputing](https://www.chem.gla.ac.uk/cronin/chemputer/) (chemical computing that could reshape molecular manufacturing), Epistemic Stack (infrastructure for verifying truth claims), and [Gevulot](https://gevulot.com/) (privacy-preserving computational infrastructure). These aren't household names, but they represent the kind of infrastructure a world with AGI will need alongside robust human coordination systems.

**Thematic patterns matter more than individual technologies.** The 39 entities cluster into 9 groups: energy systems (fusion, geothermal, space-based solar), advanced manufacturing (atomically precise, biotech, recycling), truth infrastructure (scientific publishing, epistemic stacks, prediction markets), governance protocols (competitive frameworks, dispute resolution), economic coordination (mutual credit, identity systems), ethical safeguards (AI containment, defensive technologies), AI agency architectures, human-computer interfaces, and science acceleration tools. The clustering reveals how the Foresight community is betting on *stacks*: interconnected systems that reinforce each other. Privacy infrastructure enables governance experimentation. Scientific publishing for machines accelerates AI-assisted research. Manufacturing breakthroughs require new financing mechanisms.

**A case for funding infrastructure, not the finish line.** If you're allocating capital, regulatory attention, or research priority, consider asking: "what infrastructure prevents powerful technologies from becoming centralized bottlenecks?" The entities in this report, especially those in the upper-right quadrant and the undervalued list, represent infrastructure that makes other breakthroughs safer, more distributed, and more likely to benefit humanity broadly. Many need decade-long funding timelines, patient capital, interdisciplinary teams, and regulatory frameworks designed for experimentation rather than premature standardization. They also need to be *named*: brought into policy conversations currently dominated by AGI timelines and semiconductor export controls. The future's infrastructure is being built now, often quietly. This report is one attempt to map where.
