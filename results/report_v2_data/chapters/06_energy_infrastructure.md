### Energy & Infrastructure

These three entities share a diagnosis: existing energy and governance systems move too slowly for the pace of climate change and technological opportunity. Each bypasses centralized infrastructure rather than waiting for it to improve, whether through mesh energy networks, small modular reactors, or jurisdictions that compete for climate adaptation investment. The underlying bet is that coordination problems in energy and climate will be solved by creating alternatives that make old systems obsolete.



### Decentralized Adaptive Energy Network
`Scaling Challenges` | *d/acc: 90% | Tech: 73%*

**Significance.** Centralized power grids lose up to 60% of generated electricity in transmission and distribution, and struggle with intermittent renewables. A decentralized adaptive network inverts this: AI coordinates millions of distributed sources (rooftop solar, home batteries, EVs) to trade power peer-to-peer, routing electricity based on real-time local supply and demand. The result: fuller renewable capacity use, a more resilient grid, and households with real energy control.

**Current state.** The building blocks exist separately but have not converged. [Tesla's South Australian VPP](https://www.tesla.com/en_au/sa-virtual-power-plant) connects thousands of home Powerwalls as grid-scale storage. [Power Ledger](https://www.powerledger.io) enables peer-to-peer solar trading. [AutoGrid](https://uplight.com/uplight-acquires-autogrid/) (now part of Uplight) uses ML for demand forecasting. The [Energy Web Foundation](https://www.energyweb.org) has built blockchain infrastructure for energy decentralization. [Tesla's Autobidder](https://www.tesla.com/support/energy/tesla-software/autobidder) (operational since 2020) manages grid-scale battery trading. [Sonnen Community](https://sonnengroup.com/sonnencommunity/) in Germany and [Reposit Power](https://repositpower.com/) in Australia offer early peer-to-peer models. [Vehicle-to-grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle-to-grid) capabilities are emerging as EVs become mobile grid assets. These remain isolated pilots, not the seamless self-organizing network envisioned. The biggest barrier is regulatory: electricity markets were designed for one-way power flow, and incumbent utilities have revenue models that resist democratized energy control.

**Open questions.** Whether millions of distributed devices can coordinate in real-time without centralized dispatch remains unproven at scale. A distributed network presents a large cybersecurity attack surface. Interoperability standards are fragmented ([IEEE 2030.5](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/2030.5/11216/), [OpenADR](https://www.openadr.org/), competing protocols). The likely path is gradual evolution over 10-20 years, driven by whether regulators and utilities accept business model disruption or delay it.



### Deep Fision Micro Nuclear Reactors
`Early Demonstrations` | *d/acc: 80% | Tech: 67%*

**Significance.** Modern power grids are brittle: a single storm or attack can darken entire regions for days. [Deep Fission](https://deepfission.com) is developing car-sized nuclear reactors designed to be buried a mile underground and operate autonomously for 30-50 years. The goal is mass-manufacturing nuclear power like automobiles, then deploying reactors where needed: remote communities, military bases, data centers, critical infrastructure.

**Current state.** No borehole reactor has been built at prototype scale. Deep Fission, emerging from [Intellectual Ventures](https://www.intellectualventures.com)' spinout network, competes in a crowded field of over 100 SMR ventures. [NuScale Power](https://www.nuscalepower.com) received the first NRC design certification for an SMR in 2023 (though its [Idaho project was cancelled](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/nuscale-uamps-terminate-small-modular-nuclear-reactor-smr-project-idaho/699281/) due to cost overruns). [TerraPower](https://www.terrapower.com) broke ground on its Natrium reactor in Wyoming in 2024, and [Kairos Power](https://kairospower.com) secured a construction permit for its test reactor in 2023. Deep Fission's design is more radical: sealed thorium cores that cannot melt down, fuel that cannot be weaponized, and zero above-ground footprint. The [US Nuclear Regulatory Commission](https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/new-reactors/advanced) has reformed its licensing process for advanced reactors. [Sweden's parliament voted in 2024 to expand nuclear capacity](https://www.loc.gov/item/global-legal-monitor/2024-01-29/sweden-new-rules-for-nuclear-power-plant-reactors-take-effect/), signaling a European policy shift. Competitors already have physical test facilities and are years ahead in the regulatory queue.

**Open questions.** No regulatory framework exists for autonomous underground nuclear generation. The economics are unproven: factory manufacturing at automotive scale requires gigafactory-level capital before a single unit generates revenue. Public acceptance of distributed underground nuclear installations remains untested. The thorium fuel supply chain does not yet exist.



#### Additional Entities

**Climate Adaptation Jurisdictional Arbitrage**
`Early Demonstrations` | *d/acc: 80% | Tech: N/A*
New cities and special economic zones designed to absorb climate migrants before displacement crises occur, turning forced migration into economic development. Rather than waiting for millions to overwhelm unprepared cities, this approach creates legal and economic frameworks for relocation to purpose-built communities with jobs, infrastructure, and integration pathways. Current projections suggest [200 million to 1 billion people](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2021/09/13/climate-change-could-force-216-million-people-to-migrate-within-their-own-countries-by-2050) could be displaced by 2050, and reactive humanitarian responses have consistently failed to provide dignified, economically productive outcomes.
