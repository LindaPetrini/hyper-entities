### Truth & Epistemic Infrastructure

AI-generated content and algorithmic personalization have undermined collective capacity to agree on what is true. The systems in this chapter propose new technical infrastructure: cryptographic proof systems, AI-enhanced reasoning tools, and mechanisms that make facts verifiable, arguments traceable, and epistemological processes transparent. The common thread is that trust at scale requires architecture. Better collective sense-making depends on systems that constrain manipulation, surface disagreement productively, and make the provenance of claims auditable.



### Epistemic Stack
`Early Demonstrations` | *d/acc: 85% | Tech: 63%*

**Significance.** Every day, consequential decisions are made based on unverifiable claims, from COVID treatments to climate interventions to AI safety risks. Current information systems force a choice between trusting authorities blindly or spending hours tracing sources, often hitting dead ends. The Epistemic Stack envisions transparent knowledge infrastructure where any claim (scientific, policy, or technical) can be traced through layers of evidence to raw data, with each step's credibility assessable. Coordinating around complex problems requires shared understanding of what is actually known, and misinformation thrives in opacity.

**Current state.** The building blocks exist but not the integrated system. [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org), powered by the [Allen Institute for AI](https://allenai.org), uses AI to extract metadata from over 200 million papers, creating citation graphs showing influence relationships. [OpenAlex](https://openalex.org) (launched 2022) provides an open catalog of global research outputs. [Scite.ai](https://scite.ai) (founded 2019) classifies whether citations support, contradict, or merely mention claims: a rudimentary version of claim-level evidence assessment. Wikipedia represents the closest large-scale system linking claims to sources, though without deep provenance trees. [Bloxberg](https://bloxberg.org), a research consortium running an Ethereum-compatible chain since 2019, experiments with cryptographic verification of research data. No one has assembled these into queryable provenance chains from high-level assertions down to raw observations. On the standards side, the [W3C PROV specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/) (2013) provides a data model for provenance tracking. The [Center for Open Science](https://www.cos.io) promotes transparency through the [Open Science Framework](https://osf.io). [DeSci Labs](https://desci.com) explores decentralized science infrastructure via its CODEX Protocol. [ORCID](https://orcid.org) provides persistent researcher identifiers for tracking authorship across systems. No major funder or institution has committed to building the full stack.

**Open questions.** The hardest challenge is semantic and institutional, not technical. Can AI reliably determine whether two claims support or contradict each other across disciplines? Current NLP struggles with nuanced reasoning. Will researchers adopt structured metadata standards when publication incentives reward speed over transparency? Who decides how credibility scores are calculated, and can the system avoid becoming a gatekeeping weapon against heterodox ideas? Cross-platform knowledge infrastructure also requires coordination mechanisms that do not yet exist. The governance problem must be solved before the technology problem.



#### Additional Entities

**AI-Assisted Epistemological Enhancement System**
`Early Demonstrations` | *d/acc: 80% | Tech: 77%*
Humans make consequential decisions hampered by cognitive biases, information overload, and tribal reasoning that filters out inconvenient evidence. AI-assisted epistemological systems aim to act as cognitive scaffolding, helping people forecast more accurately, synthesize contradictory evidence, and test beliefs against reality rather than ideology. Early experiments show promise in forecasting platforms such as [Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/) and in research synthesis tools, but the hardest questions remain: whether AI enhances or replaces human judgment, and who controls the epistemic infrastructure. (Note: this entity focuses on individual reasoning enhancement, whereas Epistemic Infrastructure for Truth Verification addresses systemic verification at the platform level.)

**Distributed Zero-Knowledge Security Systems**
`Scaling Challenges` | *d/acc: 80% | Tech: 67%*
These systems protect critical infrastructure from sophisticated cyber attacks by eliminating centralized points of failure. They use advanced cryptography to enable real-time threat detection across distributed networks (power grids, telecommunications) while keeping sensitive data protected during security checks. A successful breach of centralized security could cascade across entire sectors. The core challenge is making cryptographically intensive operations fast enough for real-world industrial control systems.

**Epistemic Infrastructure for Truth Verification**
`Early Demonstrations` | *d/acc: 80% | Tech: 76%*
AI systems now generate information faster than humans can verify it, eroding the ability to distinguish truth from plausible fiction when critical decisions about climate, health, and governance depend on shared facts. Epistemic infrastructure would create systematic ways to trace claims to sources, algorithmically assess credibility, and build consensus about what is true, building "supply chains for truth" at machine speed. Without this, society risks fragmenting into incompatible realities where coordination on shared problems becomes impossible. (Note: this entity addresses systemic verification infrastructure, whereas AI-Assisted Epistemological Enhancement System focuses on augmenting individual reasoning capacity.)
