### Ethics & Moral Expansion

The hyper-entities in this cluster explore technologies and frameworks designed to expand humanity's capacity for moral consideration beyond current tribes, species, and timeframes. They share a premise: moral intuitions evolved for small-scale societies and may be inadequate for planetary-scale impacts and potentially transformative AI. Together, they suggest that moral progress could be deliberately engineered through new tools for perspective-taking, value alignment, and inter-group negotiation, rather than left to cultural evolution alone.



### Expanded Moral Circle Technologies
`Foundational Research` | *d/acc: 80% | Tech: N/A*

**Significance.** Humanity is approaching a world with multiple forms of intelligence (biological, artificial, and potentially others) yet lacks tools to understand each other's experiences. Current communication technologies translate words but fail to convey the texture of consciousness itself: what it feels like to be an octopus, how an AI processes a prompt, whether a whale's song carries recognizable meaning. This gap creates ethical blindspots, making it impossible to recognize suffering or sentience in very different minds. Technologies bridging these experiential divides could enable the most significant expansion of moral consideration since the abolition movements.

**Current state.** The field is in a proof-of-concept phase for individual pieces, not the integrated vision. The [Earth Species Project](https://www.earthspecies.org) uses ML to decode animal vocalizations, while [Project CETI](https://www.projectceti.org), funded by the Audacious Project since 2020, published 2024 results showing contextual structure in sperm whale communication. Psychedelic research at [Johns Hopkins](https://www.hopkinspsychedelic.org/) and [Imperial College London](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/a-z-research/psychedelic-research-centre/) has demonstrated that psilocybin measurably increases nature-connectedness and emotional empathy. [Neuralink](https://neuralink.com) and [Synchron](https://synchron.com) are developing brain-computer interfaces, though focused on motor control rather than experiential translation. The [New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration) (2024) argued for taking seriously the possibility of consciousness in invertebrates, while [Eric Schwitzgebel](https://faculty.ucr.edu/~eschwitz/SchwitzAbs/AIRights.htm) has proposed frameworks for assessing AI moral status. The [Collective Intelligence Project](https://cip.org) explores governance structures incorporating diverse intelligence types. New Zealand granted the [Whanganui River legal personhood](https://ecojurisprudence.org/initiatives/te-awa-tupua-act-2017/) in 2017, creating precedent for non-human moral status. Still, decoding signals is not the same as understanding their phenomenology; this is what philosopher Thomas Nagel called the ["what is it like"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_Is_It_Like_to_Be_a_Bat%3F) problem.

**Open questions.** The core challenge is whether subjective experience can be translated across very different cognitive architectures. Even if researchers decode an animal's neural patterns, the conceptual apparatus to represent their phenomenology may not exist. There is no science of consciousness robust enough to determine who merits moral status; the "hard problem" remains unsolved. The technology requires neural mapping breakthroughs that may not be feasible with current physics. The coordination problem is equally severe: expanding moral circles requires simultaneous advances in consciousness science, communication technology, and legal frameworks that currently operate in separate silos. Progress is most likely through concrete demonstrations (e.g., using decoded whale communication to support legal personhood campaigns) rather than comprehensive empathy translation systems.



#### Additional Entities

**Moral Trade Civilization**
`Foundational Research` | *d/acc: 80% | Tech: N/A*
[Moral trade](https://philpapers.org/rec/ORDMT), formalized by philosopher Toby Ord, envisions systematic exchange of ethical commitments ("I'll defer to you on animal welfare if you defer to me on cultural preservation"), turning moral conflicts from zero-sum fights into negotiable trades. Today's biggest societal deadlocks fail not for lack of technical solutions but because no mechanism honors legitimate moral diversity, forcing crude majoritarian compromises. Moral trade could unlock cooperative solutions to intractable conflicts by treating ethical preferences with the sophistication that markets bring to economic preferences.

Note: while Expanded Moral Circle Technologies focuses on expanding _which_ beings receive moral consideration, Moral Trade Civilization focuses on _how_ groups with differing moral views can negotiate and cooperate despite those differences.
