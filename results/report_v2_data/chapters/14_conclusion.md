## Conclusion

The 39 hyper-entities in this report are not predictions; they are systems already organizing investment, research, and narrative around their anticipated existence. Their earliness is what makes them worth attending to. Decisions made in the next decade about which to fund, govern, and build will shape whether the technologies of the 2030s and 2040s concentrate power or distribute it.

Three patterns stand out. Epistemic infrastructure (truth verification, collective reasoning, machine-readable knowledge) is chronically underfunded relative to its importance. Energy systems show the strongest d/acc alignment in the data, suggesting the energy transition could be among the most broadly beneficial shifts of this century. Governance and coordination tools have the longest time horizons but the highest systemic leverage, and require sustained investment on timescales most funders find uncomfortable.

Of 39 entities, only 2 are near deployment; 31 are still at foundational research or early demonstrations. These systems need patient capital, cross-disciplinary collaboration, and regulatory frameworks designed for experimentation. The window for shaping them is open now. For funders, the undervalued shortlist (Chemputing, Epistemic Stack, LexCommons, Conflict De-escalation Protocol, Gevulot) is a starting point. For policymakers, the upper-right quadrant of the scatter plot identifies systems worth building regulatory runway for today. For researchers, the Open Questions sections throughout identify frontiers where basic understanding is missing or where coordination problems are more tractable than technical ones.

This research is a first pass. The pipeline (108 source documents, 300+ candidates, three-stage scoring, independent curation) is designed to be repeatable. Future iterations should expand to non-English sources, incorporate structured expert elicitation, and re-score entities over time to track how they mature.
